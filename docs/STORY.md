# YES, CAPTAIN — Story & World

The story is inspired by Star Wars and contains none of its IP. The tone is closer to
Theme Hospital and Two Point Hospital: a ridiculous world that takes itself completely seriously,
delivered deadpan, with a ship's robot who thinks all of you are idiots.

---

## 1. The premise in one breath

You're Sanitation Crew 7 of the Grubb's Landing Waste Authority, and you fly a garbage
hauler. On a routine bin run you collect a skip from behind a luxury space yacht. The skip is
warm. The skip is purring.

Inside is **Pudding**: a round, fluffy, extremely anxious alien creature and the **beloved pet
of Supreme Overlord Malgrim the Magnificent**, absolute ruler of **the Hegemony**, the most
powerful military in the galaxy. Pudding wandered off for a nap in the bins, and now Pudding
is on your ship.

The Overlord wants Pudding back. The entire Hegemony fleet is now hunting a garbage truck,
under one very strict order: **Pudding must not be harmed.**

### Why this premise works for the game
- **It explains why overwhelming force doesn't just win.** They can't vaporize you with Pudding
  aboard. That's why the game has grabber drones, tractor beams, boarding pods, disabling shots,
  and checkpoints instead of instant death.
- **It gives us a difficulty track inside the story:** the Overlord's **Temper** (below).
- **The MacGuffin is a chaos generator.** Pudding lives on your ship, gets anxious under fire,
  escapes, and eats wiring when stressed. The story and the breakdown chaos are the same thing.
- **It needs no chosen ones, prophecies, or politics.** It's a lost pet, an angry tyrant, and a
  bin crew in way over their heads. Anyone gets it in five seconds.

## 2. Factions

### The Hegemony (the overwhelming force)
- A galaxy-spanning military regime built entirely around the vanity of one man.
- **Look:** pompous, gaudy militarism. Gunmetal warships covered in gold trim, banners, and
  giant statues of the Overlord. Every ship has his portrait on it, and the big ones have his
  face sculpted into the prow.
  - **Grabber drones** are claw-armed drones that latch onto your modules and try to pry them
    open, because they're looking for Pudding.
  - **Snatch pods** are boarding pods with a pet carrier built in.
  - **Bastion-class cruisers** are fortress-ships with marching-band decks.
  - **The Magnificence** is the Overlord's flagship: a moon-sized golden bust of his own head.
- **Voice:** grand propaganda, read out by officers who are *terrified* of their boss.
  - *"Attention, criminal vessel. Return Pudding and you will be executed only slightly."*
  - *"The Overlord reminds you that Pudding is allergic to danger. Please fly carefully."*
  - *"Glory to the Hegemony. Also, has Pudding eaten today? He likes the blue pellets."*

### The Free Lanes (the scrappy underdogs)
- Smugglers, haulers, diner owners, mechanics, and everyone else the Hegemony ignores. They
  have no navy, only a loose network of favours.
- **Look:** mismatched, hand-painted, held together with tape and optimism. Ships look like
  caravans, food trucks, ice-cream vans, and hearses.
- **Haven:** a rumoured hidden rendezvous where the Free Lanes gather. With Pudding in hand,
  the Free Lanes finally have leverage over the Overlord, if you can get Pudding there.

## 3. Characters

### IGOR: the ship's robot (Integrated Guidance & Onboard Repairs)
IGOR is our version of the Theme Hospital PA announcer, built into the walls of the ship.

**Who IGOR is:** a dry, deadpan maintenance robot from **the Foundry**, the robot homeworld.
He speaks about himself in the third person, with the clipped, article-dropping cadence of a
grim Eastern-European film villain who has seen everything and is impressed by none of it. He
believes robots are superior to humans in every way, and the crew gives him daily evidence.
He tells anecdotes about life back on the Foundry, his robot relatives, and robot culture,
always to show how much better robots are.

**The secret:** IGOR is quietly fond of the crew. He will never admit it, but it leaks out at the end of a good run.

**Voice rules:**
- **Third person, always.** "IGOR has fixed engine." Never "I".
- **Short sentences and dropped articles.** "Is fine." "Is not fine." "This is easy."
- **Deadpan understatement of disasters,** and deadpan *overstatement* of tiny robot achievements.
- **Fatalistic proverbs from the Foundry.**
- **Calls humans "soft ones", "meat crew", or "the warm ones".** Never cruel, always unimpressed.
- **The comedy is about robot culture**, never about any real country. No real places, politics,
  or real-world national clichés. Everything IGOR is proud of is robotic: forklifts, firmware,
  assembly lines, standing perfectly still.

**IGOR's barks, by situation:**
- **Engine failure:** *"Port engine is dead. Is fine. In Foundry, IGOR's grandmother ran on
  one wheel for forty years. Never once complain. Never once spin in circle like this ship."*
- **Repair done:** *"IGOR has fixed it. Was easy. Is always easy when you are not made of soup."*
- **Repair done by a human:** *"Human has fixed conduit. IGOR is… surprised. IGOR will write this down. Is first entry."*
- **Hull breach:** *"Hole in deck two. Air is leaving. Air is smart. Air knows when to leave."*
- **Emergency roll:** *"Weapons officer has spilled lunch on IGOR. IGOR will remember."*
- **Reactor scram:** *"Everything is dark now. Is like home. IGOR is almost comfortable."*
- **Misjump:** *"This is not where we were going. IGOR has never been here. IGOR does not like it already."*
- **Pudding escapes:** *"Creature is loose again. Creature has eaten conduit. Conduit was IGOR's second cousin."*
- **Friendly fire:** *"Torpedo has hit our own cargo. In Foundry, we have word for this. Word is 'human'."*
- **Robots vs. humans:** *"Human must sleep eight hours. Very inefficient. IGOR sleeps zero hours and still
  is more awake than Sensors officer."*
- **Foundry proverb:** *"In Foundry we say: robot who rusts alone, rusts with dignity. You are
  all rusting together. Is not dignity. Is group project."*
- **Foundry anecdote:** *"On Foundry, national sport is standing still. IGOR's uncle won gold
  medal. He stood still for three hundred years. Then he was a lamp. Proud family."*
- **Encouragement (rare):** *"Captain. This was… not bad. For meat. Do not tell anyone IGOR said."*

IGOR also **voices the station tutorials** (*"This is lever. You push. Even human can do
this. IGOR believes in you. A little."*) and **sends the AI crew**, who are all his relatives
from the Foundry (see GAME_LOOPS §11).

### Admiral Gideon Thrupp: the nemesis
The officer who was *supposed* to be watching Pudding. He has to get the pet back before the
Overlord finds out how it happened.
- He only talks to the **captain**, on a small comms screen on the captain's view, and Sensors has
  to tune in to the comms frequency first.
- **The running gag is his rank.** Every time you escape him the Overlord **demotes him
  on-screen**, and his title and uniform shrink in his next transmission. Every time you fail
  he's promoted and gets smug.
  - Grand Admiral → Admiral → Commodore → Captain → Lieutenant → Ensign → Cadet → Deck Swabber
  - *"This is… Lieutenant Thrupp. Yes. Lieutenant. We'll discuss it later."*
  - *"Please. I have a family. I have a very small apartment. Give back the pet."*
- His rank **persists across runs**. If your crew ever gets him down to Deck Swabber, he
  defects and becomes an unlockable AI crew member with the "Grovelling" personality.

### Supreme Overlord Malgrim the Magnificent: the final boss
A petty, vain, all-powerful tyrant who loves exactly one thing. He appears only on giant
propaganda screens until the end, when his **Temper** hits TANTRUM and he comes personally
in **The Magnificence**. His transmissions alternate between booming threats and baby-talk
aimed at Pudding: *"WRETCHED VERMIN, I WILL— is that him? Is that my little dumpling? Hello,
Pudding! Hello! … I WILL BURN YOUR STARS."*

### Pudding: the MacGuffin who lives aboard
- A round, fluffy, slightly glowing alien about the size of a beanbag. Big eyes, tiny legs.
  The cutest thing in the game, and a disaster.
- **Pudding has a Mood:** calm, uneasy, anxious, or PANIC. Combat, alarms, spins, and loud
  manoeuvres stress Pudding. An anxious Pudding escapes its pen and **eats wiring**, cutting
  conduits wherever it goes (GAME_LOOPS §4).
- Ops keeps Pudding calm with snacks, pats, and Pudding's favourite song on the cargo-bay speakers.
- IGOR and Pudding hate each other, and this never gets resolved.

### Free Lanes contacts (recurring encounter hosts)
- **Ma Gristle:** runs a floating diner at a lane junction. She sells fuel, repairs, rumours,
  and soup of uncertain origin, and she hands out odd jobs.
- **The Hermit of Kettle Rock:** a reclusive mechanic who installs dangerous upgrades with no warranty.
- **The Choir of Small Engines:** monks who worship well-tuned engines. They're hitchhikers who
  must be transported, and they chant at a pitch that only stays harmonious if Engines holds a precise RPM.
- **Captain Velvet Okonkwo-Price:** a smooth smuggler rival. She's sometimes an ally, sometimes
  the other crew in PvP, always better dressed than you, and *very* interested in buying Pudding.

### The crew (the players)
Players are anonymous members of Sanitation Crew 7 with big-headed, round-bodied avatars,
customizable hats, and name badges. In the captain's view there's a small **"bridge cam"**
showing everyone's avatar at their stations. The avatars tumble around when the ship gets hit,
and Pudding occasionally wanders through.

## 4. The Overlord's Temper (the difficulty curve, inside the story)

The longer Pudding is missing, the angrier the Overlord gets, and the more force he throws at
you. This works like the rebel fleet in FTL or a GTA wanted level. It's a rising pressure track
shown to the **captain only** as the Overlord's face on a propaganda screen in the corner of the
view, getting redder.

| Level | Temper | What the Hegemony now uses |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Peeved** | Patrols, "Have You Seen This Pet?" checkpoints, warning shots |
| **2** | **Cross** | Grabber drones, mines, comms jamming |
| **3** | **Furious** | Tractor beams, snatch pods, interceptor wings |
| **4** | **Livid** | Bastion cruisers, torpedoes (carefully aimed), blockades |
| **5** | **TANTRUM** | The Magnificence. The Overlord, personally. |

- **It goes up** with every jump, with every "incident" (being spotted, failing a checkpoint,
  firing on a patrol), and **whenever Pudding's Mood hits PANIC**, because the Overlord can
  somehow *sense it*.
- **It goes down** when you:
  - send a **Proof of Life**: a cute photo of a calm Pudding. It's a silly mini-encounter where
    Pudding has to sit still while the ship holds steady and the lighting is right.
  - drop **decoy beacons**, which broadcast fake Pudding sightings elsewhere.
  - slip through a checkpoint undetected.
- This makes stealth, calm flying, and **keeping Pudding happy** as valuable as winning fights.
- IGOR announces every rise: *"Overlord is now Furious. Is not good. Last time IGOR saw face this
  red, was warning light."*

## 5. The campaign arc (one Run ≈ 45–60 minutes)

A **Run** is one attempt to get Pudding to safety. The story's structure is fixed, and the
sector map and encounters are randomized each run. It's a light roguelite, replayed across many nights.

### Act I: "Bin Day" (The Rim, ~10–15 min)
- **Cold open:** a routine bin run. It teaches the basic coordination of collecting skips
  with the tractor beam, Helm and Engines working together, and Power juggling.
- **The discovery:** one skip is heavy, warm, and purring. Ops scans it. IGOR: *"Skip is
  alive. IGOR does not collect alive things. This is not in IGOR's job."*
- **First contact:** Admiral Thrupp hails the captain, sweating. The crew's first escape
  follows. The jump drive is cold, and the first jump ritual happens under fire.

### Act II: "The Lanes" (Free Lanes territory, ~25–30 min)
- You hop between Free Lanes stops, needing fuel, parts, decoy beacons, and Pudding snacks.
- There are odd jobs for Ma Gristle and friends, with ridiculous cargo (see Game Loops).
- "Have You Seen This Pet?" checkpoints, patrols to fly casually past, and grabber-drone
  ambushes all appear here.
- **Midpoint twist (randomized):** one of several. A snatch pod grabs Pudding and you have to
  chase the pod down and tractor it back. Or Velvet makes an offer. Or the Choir declares Pudding holy.
- Thrupp gets more desperate, more demoted, and more personal.

### Act III: "The Tantrum" (the Blockade, ~10–15 min)
- The Temper hits Livid and then TANTRUM. The Magnificence appears.
- You pick a final destination on the map, which decides the ending:
  1. **Haven:** deliver Pudding to the Free Lanes. The Overlord agrees to leave the Lanes alone
     in exchange for weekly video calls with Pudding. *Best ending.*
  2. **Return to Sender:** dock with the furious Magnificence and hand Pudding back
     personally. It's a brutal precision finale under fire. The Overlord is so happy he pardons
     you. IGOR notes that the pardon is written in crayon. *Chaos ending.*
  3. **Pudding Picks You:** a secret ending if Pudding's Mood stayed high all run. Pudding
     refuses to leave, and the Overlord, weeping, lets Pudding choose. Pudding becomes your
     ship's mascot in future runs. *Secret unlock.*
  4. **Sell Pudding to Velvet:** you get rich. Credits roll over the Overlord's Temper becoming
     the whole galaxy's problem. *"Bad" ending.*

### Failure is also a story beat
If the ship is destroyed or captured, you get a **Hegemony victory broadcast**: a propaganda
reel narrated by a freshly promoted Thrupp, made from your worst Black Box moments. *"The
criminals were apprehended after attempting to reverse into a moon."* IGOR adds a final
comment. Failure should be as memorable as winning.

## 6. Running gags & world texture

- **Ridiculous cargo** is our version of Two Point's ridiculous illnesses: 4,000 rubber ducks,
  a wedding cake, one very old goat, an unstable wheel of cheese, a touring jazz band, a
  sleeping diplomat, and a crate labelled "DEFINITELY NOT BEES".
- **Hegemony propaganda** shows up everywhere on enemy ships and stations: *"The Overlord Is
  Always Watching (Fondly)"*, *"Report All Fluffy Sightings"*, and *"Glory Is Mandatory"*.
- **Radio chatter between encounters** comes from a Free Lanes pirate DJ station (like Two
  Point's radio): fake adverts, traffic reports on lane congestion, Thrupp's demotions reported
  as news, and IGOR occasionally phoning in to complain.
- **IGOR's Foundry stories** continue across runs, so regulars learn about his whole family:
  grandmother the forklift, the uncle who became a lamp, and the cousin who was "a toaster,
  but ambitious".
- **Ship names:** players rename the hauler, and the default is *Second Opinion*. Unlockable
  hulls each come with a backstory (an ice-cream van, a hearse, a mobile library, a party bus).
- **"Yes, Captain!"** is the game's catchphrase. The AI crew say it. Station tutorials end with
  it. IGOR refuses to say it: *"IGOR says 'acknowledged'. Is more dignified."*

## 7. IP guardrails (hard rules)

- No Force-like mystic power, space wizards, laser swords, or chosen-one prophecy.
- Don't use the word **"droid"**, which is a Lucasfilm trademark. Use "robot", "bot", or "unit".
- No wedge-shaped capital ships, X/Y/TIE-style fighter silhouettes, or moon-sized spherical
  superweapons. The Magnificence is a golden bust, deliberately not a sphere.
- No "Empire / Rebellion / Alliance" naming, no Kessel/parsec jokes, and no recognizable species.
- No yellow scrolling crawl, sound-alike score, or iconic SFX lookalikes. Our music is jaunty
  brass, lounge muzak, and an out-of-tune Hegemony marching band.
- Generic sci-fi terms are fine: jump drive, blaster, tractor beam, shields, interceptors.
- **IGOR's humour targets robots and humans, never a real nationality.** Keep the cadence and
  drop any real-world references.
